URI · “We” · two worlds, one body

Two civilisations on a double planet orbited each other for ten thousand years without ever becoming one. Then the Mouth came. They built this body too late to save their worlds: they carved into it everything they could carry and fled while their planets were eaten. It is all that is left of them. They live in it now, two peoples in one wandering world, crossing the cosmos to find the thing that ate their home.

Anatomy, and why it is built this way

The torso is what is left of the worlds

Basalt plates carved from both planets, gold showing in some of the cracks. The iron core of the first world was spun up into a dynamo: that is the heart, glowing through the vent in the chest, and its magnetic field is the shield.

The limbs are the second world

The second world was taken apart: its moons, ice and metal became the arms, legs and armour. Anything this size collapses into a sphere under its own gravity, so the limbs are not welded on. They float.

Coils hold it together

Active support: streams of mass moving at enormous speed, stiff like a hose under pressure, wound into glowing cyan coils around every joint. Anything this size should collapse into a sphere; the coils are why it does not.

Two cities live on it

On one shoulder, gold gothic spires; on the other, blue crystal towers. One body, two cultures. Waterfalls spill off the plateaus and weather drifts around them: they are not cockpits, they are homes.

The seam, the scar, the trophies

A straight line of white-gold light where the two worlds were joined; it only turns jagged when it is hurt. On its flank, a ring of tooth marks from the first time it met the Mouth, never repaired. In its knuckles, teeth torn from the Mouth’s rings.

The head is the capital

A small domed head inside a thin atmosphere, a single visor slit (amber on one side, blue on the other, white where they meet), a command spire, and satellites in orbit around it for scale.

Silhouette and proportions

  • Height: about 2.5 times the Planet’s diameter, slightly taller than the Witness.
  • Build: hunched and heavy, like the key art. A huge rounded torso that carries the cities on top, a small head set forward, long arms with oversized forearms and stone fists, short pillar legs that end in engine pods.
  • Coils: glowing cyan coils at the shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips and knees. Keep them calm in the neutral pose so the stone stays the main read; let them flare when it attacks.
  • Materials at pixel scale: four large readable areas only: dark basalt, old bronze bands, ice on the forearms and legs, cyan coils. Fine stone texture only at the edges, or it turns into noise.
  • Five lights define it at a glance: visor slit, chest seam, core vent, the gold city and the blue city. It must read as a humanoid at 64 pixels.
  • Weathered, never shiny: basalt and old bronze, impact scars, plates of different ages. Built in a hurry by people who were afraid.

Motion: Newton is its choreographer

  1. Every punch is paid for. When an arm goes forward, the torso rotates the other way to conserve angular momentum, then attitude thrusters fire to stop it. Windup, counter-rotation, correction burn.
  2. The engines are the telegraph. Before any heavy move, rows of engines across its back light up like continents at night. The opponent sees it coming; the player feels the load.
  3. The limbs lag. Held by mass streams, they follow the body with a delay, overshoot, and settle: heavy, springy, never instant.
  4. Impacts land on its own world. Shock rings run through its atmosphere, the cities flicker, armour plates shake loose.
  5. Input is still immediate. The visor and engines respond on the first frame; the body takes its time to follow.

Damage, defeat and comeback

  1. Armour peels. Plates of moon rock break away and drift.
  2. The lattice shows. Under the stone, the glowing grid of mass streams that holds it together.
  3. The cities go dark. District by district, and small ships flee its shoulders.
  4. The seam opens. White-gold light leaks out of the chest: the alliance itself is failing.

Defeat: the mass streams fail, and the two halves drift apart, each with its last few lights. Comeback: at the lowest health, both peoples give everything; the seam bursts open and one last joint strike becomes available.

Sound: deep stone and metal stress, mass streams singing like taut cable, engines like weather, and in the cities a faint choir, in two tunings that only resolve when it uses its ultimate.
